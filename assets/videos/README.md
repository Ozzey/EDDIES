# Videos

All clips are 1920x1080, 30 fps, H.264 (yuv420p, `+faststart`); the clips are silent, the supplementary video carries a narration track (AAC stereo, encoded at 128 kbps, about 99 kbps average because of the silent stretches; speech at -16 LUFS, no music); every clip is under 25 MB, the supplementary video under 60 MB. Every number in a caption or card comes from the paper (`main.tex`, Tables 1-3, Fig. 4 and 8); the overlays are rendered by the scripts described at the end.

## Overlay language

The same elements appear on every clip, in the paper's colour scheme (navy `#221b45` text, blue `#2e90ef` Franka / Eddies / tube, orange `#f39a2b` G1, violet `#6a1fd6` LEAP hand / certified tube radius / speed badge, slate `#2e4b6c` Aero hand, red `#d6452b` certified stop):

1. **Eddie stream bar** (bottom of the MuJoCo and robot-camera clips, top of the side-view robot clips): one rounded chip per Eddie of the stream, `primitive` over `frame`, in stream order. The active Eddie is filled in the robot's colour, finished Eddies are navy-outlined, pending ones grey, a refused Eddie is red. The thin violet bar in each chip is the Eddie's certified tube radius r_i drawn against mu (full chip width = mu; the value is printed in cm). Radii are the plan tubes of the task's certificate (`results/embodiments/embodiments.json`, `plan_tube_cm`; for the 2x clip the 2x values of `results/gate/gate_eddy.json`; the real-robot clips use the MuJoCo certificate, as the robot did). Streams longer than ten Eddies show a nine-chip window with `n done` / `+n more` tags.
2. **Gate badge** with the check `||S eta_hat|| + eps ||S W^-1/2|| <= mu`: `SEND` (blue, check mark) while the running Eddie's next command passes, `CERTIFIED STOP` (red, pulsing, plus a red frame border) while it does not; the second line gives the reason and, on the robot, the running hold time. Grey `GATE` means no command is being checked (before the stream starts, while the plan is refused and the ungated run is shown for reference, or while the cube is being moved before the run).
3. **Live plot** (MuJoCo clips only, bottom right): measured hand error `||S(z - e)||` in cm, the distance between the measured and the commanded hand position from the render's `trajectory.npz` (`z`, `e`, converted to metres with the manifest's embedding bounds), against the certified tube radius of the active Eddie (blue step area) and mu (dashed red); the window is the last 4 s. This is the error to the command, not to the model equilibrium z*(e) of the paper's certificate; on the Franka the two are a median 0.2 cm apart and the error to the command stays within the tube on 99.9% of test steps (paper, Appendix on the equilibrium offset). Red vertical ticks mark the perturbation events; a red tick at the first refused command marks a stop.
4. **Phase caption** (navy box, white text, lower left): what the stream does now, on the real clips with the time code `t` (t = 0 when the arm starts); **summary caption** (white box, navy text, blue bar) with the paper's numbers, in the supplementary video, in the 2x clip and on the four plan-refused clips (top left on the G1 drawer clip, where the humanoid fills the lower left; lower left elsewhere);**event flash** (red box) for the push and the dropout.
5. **Title chip** (task, robot, engine or `real robot`) with a violet speed badge (`x1.5`, `x2`, `x2.5`, `x3`) whenever the clip is not played at 1x.

**Which gate the badge shows.** On the task clips (MuJoCo and real robot) `SEND` / `CERTIFIED STOP` is the self-calibrating gate's next-command check, the gate the paper runs online (Algorithm 1; the *self-calibrating gate* columns of Tables 1, 3 and D.1). The 2x clip (`sim/franka_cube_stack_rho2.mp4`) instead shows the fixed-calibration *plan* check, which tests every planned Eddie's tube against mu before motion (the `fixed` column of Table D.1). The two differ on four task clips: the plan check refuses the Franka drawer (`reach:handle_pre` tube 5.2 cm > 5 cm), the G1 drawer (`pull:drawer` 8.3 cm > 7.5 cm), the LEAP wheel (`turn:wheel` 11.9 cm > 5 cm) and the Aero wheel (`turn:wheel` 6.5 cm > 5 cm) before motion (Table D.1: 0 of 200 runs finished under the fixed gate), while the self-calibrating gate lets 200, 197, 172 and 200 of 200 runs finish. Those four clips carry a summary caption saying so, and their chips draw the > mu tube radii as full-width violet bars. (G1 peg insertion is also refused by the fixed gate, by its hand-over check: none of its tubes exceeds 7.5 cm, so its clip carries no such caption.)

The MuJoCo frames are rendered without the recorder's debug status box (the top left of the frame is clear) and keep its close-up inset (top right); the robot-camera feed keeps its own status line.

**Picture-in-picture synchronisation (side-view robot clips).** The robot-camera feeds (`STACK.mp4`, `Perturbation-1.mp4`) are written at a nominal 15 fps but captured at a varying rate (14.2 and 14.8 frames per real second on average, with per-second variation of 11-16 frames), so played at 15 fps they drift against the 60 fps side view. Each feed carries its own wall clock (HH:MM:SS) in its status line; the frame at which the seconds digit changes was found for every second by frame differencing of the digit region (61 ticks in `Perturbation-1.mp4`, 51 in `STACK.mp4`, no skipped second: the digit sequence was checked against templates), which gives a piecewise-linear map from feed frame to real time with one knot per second. The constant offset between that clock and the side view was measured on events visible in both views, found independently in each by frame differencing of the arm region: first arm motion, the arm resuming after the certified stop, the end of the descent to the cube, lift-off and the start of the retreat (perturbation run: offsets 13.51, 13.51, 13.88, 13.47, 13.47 s, median 13.51 s, residuals of the sharp onsets within 0.04 s); first arm motion, descent, lift and retreat (nominal run: 8.30, 8.53, 8.25, 8.20 s, median 8.28 s). A whole-signal cross-correlation of the two motion-energy traces gives the same offsets within 0.1 s (13.52 and 8.18 s). The feed is then resampled frame-exactly: every output frame shows the feed frame captured at that side-view instant (clip speed changes included), so the two views coincide to within one feed frame (0.07 s) plus the anchor residual. The alignment was checked by viewing the side view and the inset at every anchor (first motion, stop begins, pushes during the stop, resume, grasp, lift-off, release; and first motion, grasp, lift, place, release, retreat). The phase labels in the feed's status line (`DESCEND`, `GRASP`, ...) are the controller's own and can change up to 0.4 s after the motion they name is visible (the `DESCEND` -> `GRASP` change at the grasp of the perturbation run); the sharp events (lift-off, release) agree within 0.2 s.

## How the overlays were made

Overlay frames (RGBA PNG, 15 fps) are drawn with Pillow from a per-clip spec (Eddie start times from `trajectory.npz`, from the shot sheet of the side views or from the feed's phase labels; tube radii from the result JSONs; captions, stops and events), the base clip is trimmed, re-timed and padded with ffmpeg (`trim`, `setpts`, `tpad` for the held frame, `pad` or `scale`, event-aligned picture-in-picture by piecewise-linear retiming of the feed), and the two are composited with `overlay=format=rgb` at crf 20 (`libx264`, preset medium, `+faststart`). The supplementary video concatenates the card clips (PNG cards with 0.4 s fades, crf 18) and the clip variants with summary captions, re-encoded once at crf 20. The scripts (`ovlib.py`, `make_sim.py`, `make_real.py`, `make_feed.py`, `cards.py`, `make_supp.py`, `make_assets.py`, `qa_final.py`) live in the build scratch folder of the video stage and are reproducible from the sources above; the narrated cut adds the final pass's `tts.py` / `tts_fix.py` (edge-tts calls, loudnorm, padding), `make_real2.py` / `make_real3.py` (clock-synchronised picture-in-picture, segment maps of the supplementary variants), `make_montage.py`, `cards2.py` / `cards_fix.py` (QR end card, card durations, the three-stage Eddie segment) and `assemble.py` / `assemble_fix.py` (concatenation, narration placement, AAC mix).
